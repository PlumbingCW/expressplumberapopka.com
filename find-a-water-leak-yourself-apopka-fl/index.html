How to Find a Water Leak Yourself in Your Apopka, FL Home.

You can find many leaks yourself with a few free checks and a little patience. Starting there saves time and money, and it gives the plumber better information when you do need help.

This guide walks through a simple order of checks, from the meter to the water heater.

Step one: the meter test.

Turn off every tap and appliance, including the ice maker and the irrigation system. Look at your water meter. Most have a small flow indicator, a triangle or a dial, that moves when any water passes. If it moves with everything off, water is leaking somewhere.

Write down the reading, wait fifteen minutes with nothing running and read it again. A change confirms a leak and gives you a rough idea of its size.

1

A simple order of checks.

Work from the easy checks to the harder ones.

Toilets.Dye test in the tank.Silent flapper leaks.

2

Check the toilets.

Toilets are the most common source of hidden water loss. Add food coloring to each tank, wait fifteen minutes and look in the bowl. Color means a leaking flapper. Our toilet repair page covers the fix.

3

Walk the house with your hands.

Water marks and soft spots show where water has been. Check ceilings under bathrooms, the walls behind sinks and the floor near the dishwasher and washing machine. Press on baseboards and flooring for soft, swollen or warped spots. Look inside cabinets with a flashlight.

4

Check outside.

Look around the yard for wet spots, unusually green strips of grass and sunken ground. Check outdoor faucets and the area near the meter. If the house valve test shows the meter still moves with the house shut off, the leak is between the meter and the house. See water service line repair.

5

Signs that point to a slab leak.

A warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water when everything is off and a hot water bill that has jumped suggest a leak under the slab. Do not break anything yourself. A plumber can find it with tools. See slab leak repair.

6

When to stop and call.

Call a plumber when:

  1. The meter still moves after you have checked toilets, sinks and irrigation.
  2. You see water you cannot trace or a ceiling that is bulging.
  3. You hear water in a wall or under the floor.
  4. The leak involves sewage or a failed water heater.
  5. You are unsure and the bill keeps climbing. See leak detection.
7

Check the water heater and appliances.

Look at the floor around the water heater for rust or dampness, and check the relief valve discharge pipe for drips. Pull the washing machine and dishwasher forward if you can and look at their supply hoses. Braided hoses can bulge or rust at the connection, and replacing them is a cheap way to avoid a flood. Check the ice maker line behind the refrigerator as well.

8

Keep a simple log.

Write down the meter reading, the date and the time each time you check. A log shows whether the leak is steady or intermittent, and it helps the plumber choose the right tools. Photographs of the meter, any wet spots and your water bills support an insurance claim if damage has already occurred.

9

What not to do.

Do not cut into walls or break floors to look for a leak, because you may hit a live pipe or an electrical line. Do not ignore a wet spot because it looks small, since water often travels before it shows. And do not leave a confirmed leak for later. Even a slow leak can rot framing and feed mold, so call a plumber once you have confirmed it.

Tools and records for Apopka homeowners.

The City of Apopka's website has utility billing pages, which let you look at your use over time. A bill that jumps with no change in habits is a good reason to start these checks. Keep photos of the meter and the dates in case you need to explain a high bill later.

Apopka gets about 50 inches of rain a year, per climate data for the city, so wet soil outdoors is not always a leak. Check after several dry days before you decide.

Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Apopka, FL.

The water service line is the buried pipe that carries water from the meter to your house. When it leaks, the yard turns soggy and bills climb, so call early.

The service line is easy to forget because it is buried. It runs from the meter to the home, and a break there can waste a lot of water before anyone sees it. A wet patch in the yard, a lush stripe of grass and a dip in pressure are common signs.

A plumber locates the break and explains the options, and you should ask for a price before digging in Apopka, FL. Where the city's responsibility ends and yours begins is worth confirming, so we suggest asking your utility.

1

Yard leak or no water: do this first.

Take these steps.

Check the meter.

With indoor water off, a moving meter points to a leak between the meter and the house.

Close the house valve.

If a valve exists at the home, closing it keeps the leak from feeding the house.

Mark the wet spot.

Flag the soggy area so the plumbers can start there.

Call right away.

A buried leak can undermine soil and driveways.

2

Signs of a service line leak.

A constantly wet or sunken yard spot, a sudden pressure drop, a high water bill with no explanation and the sound of water near the meter are the main signs. Water that appears at the surface may be a distance from the true break, so a leak detection pass can save digging.

If the meter does not move when the house valve is closed, the leak is probably inside the home instead.

4

Who owns which part.

Generally the utility owns the pipe up to the meter, and the homeowner owns it from the meter to the house. Rules vary, so call your water utility and ask before you hire anyone. The plumber can explain what to ask.

5

Roots, soil and pipe material.

Older service lines may be galvanized steel or early plastic that has aged. Roots and shifting soil can pull joints apart, and heavy loads over the pipe can crush it. The new line is usually PEX, copper or a rated plastic, and the plumber explains the choice.

6

Checking the meter yourself.

Most meters have a small flow indicator, a triangle or a dial that turns when any water moves. Close every tap, wait a minute and look. If it turns, note the reading, wait fifteen minutes with nothing running, and read again. The difference tells you how fast water is leaking.

Share that reading when you call. It helps the plumbers arrive with the right equipment.

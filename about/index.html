1

How a call works.

The same steps every time.

  1. You call. The person who answers asks what you see.
  2. You ask about timing. Ask who will do the work and when they can arrive.
  3. You ask about price. Ask for the price before any work starts.
  4. You decide. Nothing should begin until you agree, and you can decline after a diagnosis.
2

How to verify any plumber's license.

In Florida, the Department of Business and Professional Regulation runs MyFloridaLicense.com, which lets you look up a licensee by name or license number. It is a good habit to check any contractor before you let them into your home, including whoever comes to your house. Ask the plumber for their license number and look it up.

3

What we do and do not do.

We handle water heaters, drains, sewer lines, leaks, fixtures and pipe work, listed on our services page. For work that needs a specialist, such as pool equipment or well pumps, we say so and tell you honestly if we are not the right fit.

We do not use forms or booking portals. Calling is faster for an emergency, and a person can ask the right follow-up question.

4

How the phone line works.

A phone line lets you describe the problem and ask about the nearest available plumber. The person who answers may not be the person who does the work, so ask on the first call who that will be and when.

What stays constant is the process. You describe the problem, you ask about timing and price, the plumber diagnoses the work and you decide. If the quote is more than you want to spend, you can say no after the diagnosis.

5

What we ask of you.

Tell the person who answers the truth about what you see, including whether water is clean or sewage and whether you have touched anything. Close the main water valve if you can do so safely. Make sure someone over 18 is home to let the plumber in, and tell us about pets, gate codes and any parking restrictions.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before you call us. A plumber can help once everyone is safe.

6

Local knowledge across Apopka.

Apopka was chartered as a town in 1882 and has grown from a citrus and foliage community into a city of more than 54,000 people at the 2020 census. The blocks near Main Street and 5th Street include some of the city's oldest homes, and communities such as Wekiva Club and Wekiva Crossing were built later. We do not assume what is inside a house from its age, and a plumber who looks first is better than one who guesses.

7

Honest limits.

We do not promise a fixed arrival time, because traffic and demand change by the hour. We do not post flat prices, because every job is different. And we do not claim to cover every trade. For pool equipment, well pumps or major structural work, we tell you honestly if another trade is the better choice.

Water Softener and Filtration in Apopka, FL.

Water softener and filtration systems treat what comes out of your taps, from mineral scale to chlorine taste. In Apopka we start with a tap test, so you buy equipment for the problem you actually have.

Treatment equipment works best when it is matched to a real result. A softener helps with mineral scale, while a carbon filter helps with taste and odor, and neither fixes every complaint. That is why we begin with testing, not a sales pitch.

In Apopka, FL we do not quote a hardness number from memory. Your water supplier publishes an annual water quality report, and a simple tap test at your house gives a result for your tap.

1

Signs your water needs treatment.

White crust on faucets and showerheads, spots on dishes, stiff laundry and a film in the shower point to minerals. A rotten egg smell, a metal taste or orange stains point to something else, and the fix is different. Describe what you see when you call.

How we choose a system.

The process is short.

  1. Test the tap. A simple home test or a lab sample gives a real result.
  2. Read the report. Your utility's annual quality report shows what is in the supply.
  3. Pick the fit. We recommend only what addresses the result.
  4. Install and set up. The system is plumbed in, tested and explained to you.

Not sure what is in your water? Call us and ask about a tap test.

4

Well water and homes outside city service.

Some properties on the edges of Apopka and in nearby towns use a private well, and well water can differ a great deal from city water. A lab test is the right starting point, and treatment may need to cover iron, sulfur or bacteria. See our Zellwood page for rural properties.

5

Maintenance and what to expect.

A softener needs salt refills and an occasional cleaning of the brine tank. Filters need new cartridges on a schedule. Ask for a simple written schedule when the system is installed, so you know what to do and when.

6

Water heaters and treated water.

Scale shortens the life of a water heater and clogs tankless units. If you own or plan to buy a tankless heater, treating the water can protect it, and that is worth discussing at install time.

7

Drinking water versus whole-house treatment.

Whole-house systems treat every tap, which helps fixtures, appliances and laundry. A drinking-water filter at the kitchen sink treats only what you drink and cook with. Many homes use both, and the choice depends on the test result and the budget.

Be wary of any quote given before a test. A salesperson who names a system without checking your water is guessing, and a guess can leave you with equipment that solves the wrong problem.

8

Installation and drain needs.

A softener needs a drain for its regeneration cycle, a nearby outlet and room beside the main supply line. The plumber checks that the space and drain are right before ordering anything, and fits a bypass valve so the system can be isolated for service.

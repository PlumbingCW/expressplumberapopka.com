A tankless water heater warms water on demand instead of storing it. Plumbers install, repair and descale these units, and size them so the first shower of the morning is still hot.

Tankless water heater service in Apopka, FL covers three jobs: new installation, repair of error codes and scale, and the annual flush that keeps the heat exchanger clean. Each starts with a look at your actual demand.

If your tank has already failed and you want to move to tankless, we will price that alongside a standard replacement so you can see the full difference.

1

How to size a tankless unit.

Sizing depends on two numbers: the flow rate you need in gallons per minute and the temperature rise from your incoming water to your set temperature. Add up the fixtures that run at once, such as a shower, a dishwasher and a kitchen tap.

A unit that is too small gives you lukewarm water when two fixtures run together. A unit that is too large costs more and may need a larger gas line. We measure rather than guess.

2

Gas line and venting.

Gas tankless units need a gas line sized for the burner and a sealed vent to the outside. Replacing a tank with a tankless unit often means a larger gas line and different vent materials.

Electric tankless models draw a lot of power and may need a heavy circuit, so the plumber and an electrician check your panel before the quote.

3

Flushing and descaling.

Mineral scale builds up inside the heat exchanger and cuts flow and efficiency. Many manufacturers recommend a periodic flush with a descaling solution, often once a year, depending on water quality.

We cannot quote your water hardness without a test, so we ask the plumber to test your tap before recommending a schedule. You can also ask your water utility for its annual water quality report.

5

Repair or replace a tankless unit.

Tankless units can last well beyond a storage tank when maintained, so repair is often worth it. A flush, a sensor or a vent fix usually restores performance. When the heat exchanger itself has failed, replacement is the realistic option. The plumber tells you which applies before any work starts.

6

What affects tankless costs.

Tankless jobs vary more than tank jobs.

Unit capacity.
Larger units for bigger households cost more.
Gas line.
A larger gas line or longer run adds labor and material.
Venting.
Special vent pipe and wall penetrations are priced per foot.
Water treatment.
A softener or filter ahead of the heater may be worth adding.
Service plan.
Annual flushes are scheduled work and priced separately from repairs.

Check the manufacturer's warranty terms. Some require periodic flushing to stay valid, so keep records of each service.

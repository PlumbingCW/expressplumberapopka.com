Emergency Hydro Jetting in Apopka, FL.

Hydro jetting uses a high-pressure water stream from a hose and nozzle to scour grease, scale and roots from the inside of a drain or sewer pipe. Plumbers use a jetter for backups that a cable could not fix.

If a cable machine opened your drain and it clogged again within weeks, jetting is the next step. The nozzle cuts the buildup off the pipe wall instead of poking one hole through it, which is why the result lasts longer.

Most hydro jetting calls in Apopka, FL begin as a backup. We confirm the pipe can take the pressure first, usually with a camera, and then clear the line. If the pipe is damaged, we tell you before anything is jetted.

1

Main line backing up: what to do first.

A backed-up main line is an emergency. Do these while the plumbers is on the way.

Stop all water use.

No flushing, laundry or dishwasher. Every gallon added makes the backup worse.

Find the cleanout.

A capped pipe in the yard or at the house edge gives the plumber direct access, so clear brush around it.

Keep people away.

Backed-up sewage carries bacteria. Keep children and pets out of the affected rooms.

Document for insurance.

Photograph the water and the affected floors before cleanup begins.

2

When a cable is not enough.

A cable clears a path, but a pipe full of grease cakes back almost immediately. Cable work on a line with roots can also leave the root mass partly in place, so the stoppage returns.

Hydro jetting fits when the cause is buildup along the whole pipe, when roots are thick, or when scale has narrowed an older line. In those cases a jetted line often runs clear for a long time.

3

Will jetting damage my pipes?

Jetting is safe for sound pipe, but a cracked, offset or collapsed line can be made worse. That is why we run a camera inspection first on any line with a history of trouble.

If the camera shows a broken section, jetting stops and the repair path is discussed instead, usually under sewer line repair. Honest limits matter here, and we state them up front.

5

Roots, grease and scale: what jetting removes.

Roots enter through joints and keep growing toward the water. A jet nozzle with cutting heads removes them, but if the joint stays open they will return, which is why we recommend repairing the opening when the camera shows one.

Grease and scale form a hard coating on the walls over many years. Jetting is the most reliable way to take that coating off, and it is a routine step before pipe lining in older lines.

6

Restaurants and heavy-use properties.

Kitchens produce grease continuously, and a grease-heavy line can clog every few months. Scheduled jetting for restaurants, apartments and offices keeps lines clear and avoids emergency calls. Our commercial plumbing page covers after-hours service for businesses.

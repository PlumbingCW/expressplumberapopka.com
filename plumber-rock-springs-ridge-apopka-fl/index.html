A community built in phases.

Rock Springs Ridge sits between Rock Springs Road on the east and Jason Dwelley Parkway on the west. A 2017 report in the Apopka Chief, citing a real estate website, put the subdivision at about 1,050 homes, with construction that began in 1998 and was still going on in 2017. The same article described a debate among residents about a former golf course inside the community.

The practical point is that a subdivision built over two decades or more does not have one house age or one set of fixtures. A home in one phase may have been finished years before a home on the next street. We do not guess a house's age from the neighborhood, and you can look up the year built for your own parcel on the Orange County Property Appraiser site.

1

How to find out what you have.

If you are trying to work out what is in your walls, there are better sources than a guess. The year built from the property appraiser is a starting point. Your closing documents or any past inspection report may name the pipe material and the water heater. The label on the water heater shows the manufacturer and the serial number, which tells you its age.

When the plumber arrives, ask him to tell you what he sees, such as the material of the visible supply lines and the shutoff valves. That is more reliable than any general rule, and it helps you plan before something fails.

3

Homeowners association and access.

Many larger subdivisions have a homeowners association, and some have gated sections. We do not know the rules for your section, so check with the association if you plan visible exterior work. When you call us, mention any gate code or access instructions, and say if you want the plumber to call you from the street.

If your home is part of a section that is still being built or has a builder warranty, call the builder first for anything the warranty may cover.

4

Utilities and permits.

Water and sewer service in this part of Apopka comes through the City of Apopka. If you are unsure whether a problem is on your side of the meter, call the city and ask where its responsibility ends. For permits on larger work, such as moving a water heater or changing drain lines, ask the City of Apopka building division what applies. Our plumber can describe what is typical, and the building office decides what is required.

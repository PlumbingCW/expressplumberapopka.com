Faucet Repair in Apopka, FL.

Faucet repair replaces the worn cartridge, washer, seat or supply line that makes a tap drip, spray or lose pressure. It is a small job that saves water and prevents cabinet damage in Apopka homes.

A dripping faucet seems harmless, but a drop per second adds up to thousands of gallons a year. A leak under the sink is worse, because it rots the cabinet floor before anyone looks.

We repair most faucets in one visit in Apopka, FL. If the faucet is too worn or the parts are no longer made, we say so and quote a replacement.

1

Why faucets drip.

Inside a faucet, a rubber washer or ceramic cartridge seals the water. Over time that seal wears, and mineral buildup from hard water speeds the wear. The faucet drips because the seal no longer closes fully.

Compression faucets with separate hot and cold handles use washers. Single-handle faucets use cartridges or ball assemblies. The plumber identifies the type and brings the matching part.

2

Faucet types and common repairs.

The repair depends on the design.

Pull-down sprayer.Weak or leaking spray.New hose or diverter.
3

Low pressure and sputtering.

If one faucet has weak flow and the others are fine, the aerator is often clogged with mineral grit. Unscrewing it and soaking it in vinegar can restore flow. If every tap is weak, the cause may be the main shutoff, a pressure regulator or a failing supply pipe, which the plumber can check.

4

Leaks under the sink.

Check the cabinet for dampness. Loose supply lines, worn shutoff valves and a faulty drain connection all cause leaks that do not show at the faucet. Close the shutoff valves under the sink, dry the area and watch for the source to reappear.

If the leak is behind a wall or you cannot find it, see leak detection.

5

Outdoor faucets and hose bibs.

An exterior spigot that drips at the handle or leaks inside the wall needs prompt attention. A freeze can crack the pipe behind it, and Apopka's record low of 15 degrees is a reminder that cold snaps happen. Wrap exposed faucets ahead of a forecast freeze, and shut off the interior valve if your home has one.

6

Repair or replace.

Faucets with a corroded body or discontinued parts cost more to repair than to replace. A new faucet also lets you upgrade to a lower-flow model. The plumber compares both options and quotes each before you decide.

7

Kitchen and bathroom differences.

Kitchen faucets work harder, with pull-down sprayers, long hoses and constant handle movement. Bathroom faucets drip more often from worn cartridges and clogged aerators. We carry parts for both, and the plumber can tell you which repairs are worth it.

Hard water and faucet wear.

Mineral buildup shortens faucet life and clogs aerators. If you see white crust on fixtures, a softener or filter may be worth a look, and a simple tap test is the way to find out what is in your water.

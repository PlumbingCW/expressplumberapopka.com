Sewer Camera Inspection in Apopka, FL.

A sewer camera inspection feeds a waterproof camera down your drain line and records the pipe's condition on video. It is the fastest way to learn what is actually wrong, and a smart step before buying an older Apopka home.

Digging blind is expensive. A sewer camera shows cracks, roots, belly sections, offsets and grease before any repair is discussed. That is why plumbers lead with it on repeat clogs and why home buyers order it before closing.

We schedule inspections ahead in Apopka, FL, and they usually take under an hour. If the camera shows a problem that is also an active backup, we move to repair or jetting on the same visit when possible.

1

What the camera finds.

The picture shows the pipe interior in real time. Roots look like fine white hair entering at a joint. Cracks appear as dark lines, offsets as a step where two pipe sections no longer line up, and a belly as standing water in a pipe that should drain dry.

The plumber narrates what they see and gives you the video. If the line is clean, you know that too, and can stop worrying about a clog that may be a plumbing fixture problem instead.

2

How an inspection works.

The process is simple and predictable.

  1. Locate the cleanout. The camera enters through a cleanout or a pulled toilet.
  2. Run the line. The operator feeds the camera toward the city connection while recording.
  3. Mark problem spots. A locator pinpoints depth and position for any defect.
  4. Review the video. You see the footage and a plain-language summary.

3

Buying an older home.

A home inspector does not usually run a sewer camera. If you are buying a house near downtown Apopka built decades ago, cast iron or clay pipe can hide serious problems. A pre-closing inspection tells you whether the line needs work and gives you a basis for negotiating.

Sellers also benefit, since a clean video report removes a common point of doubt.

4

When to order an inspection.

These situations justify one.

Situation.Why a camera helps.
Repeat clogs in the main line.Shows whether cause is grease, roots or damage.
Buying or selling a home.Documents pipe condition before closing.
Before a remodel or driveway work.Avoids damaging a hidden or fragile line.
After a cable or jetting pass.Confirms the pipe was cleared and undamaged.
5

What an inspection cannot do.

A camera shows the inside of the pipe, not the soil around it. It cannot tell you why a section failed, and a dark section of pipe can hide small defects. A smoke or dye test is sometimes added when the question is where a leak exits.

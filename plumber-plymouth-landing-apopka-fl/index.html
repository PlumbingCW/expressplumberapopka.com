Check your address first.

Plymouth Landing is described by neighborhood listing sites as a residential community of single-family homes in the 32712 area on Plymouth Sorrento Road. Those sites aggregate third-party data, and we have not verified the details, so treat them as a rough guide. In particular, a mailing address that says Apopka does not always mean the property is inside the city limits.

This matters because water, sewer and permits can come from different places depending on the jurisdiction. If your home is outside the city, you may have a different utility, and in some areas a home may use a private well or septic system rather than city service.

1

How to confirm who serves your home.

These steps take a few minutes and prevent a lot of confusion later.

  1. Find your parcel. Search your address on the Orange County Property Appraiser site, which lists the parcel and its jurisdiction.
  2. Check your water bill. The name on the bill tells you whether service comes from the City of Apopka or another provider.
  3. Look for a septic tank. If you have no sewer bill, ask whether the home is on septic and where the tank lies.
  4. Ask about permits. Call the City of Apopka building division or Orange County to find out who issues permits for your address.
  5. Tell us when you call. Give the plumber what you found, because it changes how he approaches the job.
2

What the plumber can and cannot tell you.

A plumber can look at the equipment on your side of the meter and describe what he sees. He cannot decide your jurisdiction or issue a permit, and we do not guess about the age or condition of a house from its neighborhood. One listing site reports that the community dates to about 2000 and was completed around 2004, but we cannot confirm that, and the year built on the property appraiser site is a better source for your parcel.

If you want a general idea of what to expect from an older or newer home, ask the plumber when he arrives, and let him tell you what he finds.

Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Apopka, FL.

Water heater replacement is the removal of a failed or end-of-life tank and the installation of a new unit sized for the household. Ask about timing for a planned replacement, and call right away for a flooding tank.

A tank that has failed is a replacement job, because corroded steel cannot be patched. If yours is leaking from the body, you are in the right place, and the first step is to cut the power or gas and close the cold water inlet.

We replace electric and gas storage heaters, and when the budget allows we can also move you to a tankless unit. You will see the price for each choice before anyone starts.

1

Tank failed and water spreading: what to do first.

Do these in order while the plumber is on the way.

Shut off power or gas.

Use the breaker or the gas valve on the heater, so nothing heats an empty tank.

Close the inlet.

The cold water valve above the tank stops the flood at its source.

Protect the floor.

Move anything stored near the heater and put towels or a wet vac to work.

Photograph the damage.

Pictures of the heater, its label and the wet area help with an insurance claim.

2

Signs the heater is at the end of its life.

Most storage tanks last roughly eight to twelve years. A heater past ten years with rust-colored water, a rumbling sound or moisture at the base is close to the end.

Replacement beats another repair when the tank wall is the problem. A new element or thermostat is worth fitting to a tank that is otherwise sound, and a patch on a corroded tank is not.

3

Choosing the size and fuel type.

Tank size follows the household. A common range is 40 gallons for one or two people and 50 to 80 gallons for larger families, but the plumber sizes it by looking at your actual use, not a guess.

Gas heaters recover faster and cost less to run where gas is available. Electric heaters are simpler and are common in Florida homes without gas service. A heat pump water heater costs more up front and uses much less electricity, and we can explain the trade-off for your house.

4

Here is how the three options compare for a typical Apopka home.

5

What happens on replacement day.

The plumbers shuts off water and power or gas, drains the old tank and disconnects it. A new unit goes in with fresh flexible connectors, a new relief valve and drain line, and an expansion tank when the system needs one.

Before leaving, the plumber fills the tank, tests for leaks, checks venting on gas units and explains the settings. The old tank leaves with us. Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Apopka building division whether your replacement needs one, and your plumber will tell you which paperwork applies.

6

What affects the price of a replacement.

The total depends on these items, and you see them in the quote.

Tank size and brand.
Capacity and efficiency ratings move the price more than anything else.
Fuel and venting.
Gas units may need new vent pipe, and electric units may need new wiring.
Code updates.
Replacing a heater can trigger upgrades, such as a new pan, drain line or expansion tank.
Location.
Garage installs are simpler than attic or tight closet installs.
Emergency timing.
A midnight flood call can price differently, and you hear that first.

Insurance usually pays for water damage from a sudden tank failure but not the heater itself. Photograph the label and the damage before cleanup.

Standards and sources. Gas water heater work follows the manufacturer's installation instructions and the National Fuel Gas Code (NFPA 54), and plumbing work in Florida falls under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing. We cite these by name and do not quote section numbers from memory, so ask the City of Apopka building division which requirements apply to your address.

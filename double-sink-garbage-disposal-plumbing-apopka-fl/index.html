Plumbing a Double Sink With a Garbage Disposal in Apopka, FL.

A double kitchen sink with a garbage disposal looks simple from above, but the pipes underneath have to be laid out the right way or one bowl will back up into the other. If your sink gurgles, drains slowly or leaks under the cabinet, the layout is a good place to start.

This guide explains the parts, the standard arrangement and the problems that show up when something is wrong.

How the two bowls share one drain.

Each bowl has its own drain opening, and the two join into a single trap and drain line that leads into the wall. The disposal usually mounts under one bowl, and a short pipe called a continuous waste connects the other bowl to it, so both drain through the disposal's outlet.

That shared outlet is why a clog or a bad slope affects both bowls at once. If one side fills while the other drains, the pipe between them is a suspect.

2

The correct layout in plain terms.

Both bowls drain toward one trap, with pipe that slopes gently downhill all the way to the wall. The trap should sit below the outlet and connect to a vent through the wall, so that air can enter behind the water and keep the sink from gurgling.

The dishwasher drain hose connects high on the disposal or the tailpiece. Many plumbers form a high loop in the hose to stop dirty water from draining backward into the dishwasher.

3

Common problems and what they mean.

A sink that drains slowly on both sides usually has a clog past the trap. A sink that fills one bowl while draining the other points to a clog or a bad slope in the connecting pipe. A leak that appears only when the disposal runs is usually a loose or worn gasket on the disposal itself.

If the dishwasher fills the sink with dirty water, check whether the knockout plug was removed when the disposal was installed. A plug left in place blocks the dishwasher drain entirely.

4

Why the vent matters.

Without a working vent, the drain cannot breathe. Water in the pipe pulls on the trap and can siphon it dry, which lets sewer gas into the kitchen. A gurgle that comes from the sink when another fixture drains is a clue that the vent is partly blocked.

5

What you can check yourself.

Safe checks before you call a plumber.

  1. Run each bowl separately. Fill one bowl and release it, then do the other. Note which one backs up.
  2. Look under the sink. Check for drips at the trap nuts, the flange and the disposal.
  3. Switch off the disposal. Never put a hand into it.
  4. Check the dishwasher hose. Look for the high loop and a clean connection.
  5. Skip chemical cleaners. They are unsafe around disposals and do not clear a bad layout.
6

When a repair is a layout problem.

If the sink drains poorly after every cleaning, the layout may be at fault, for instance a trap that is too low, a long flat run or a wrong connection. A plumber can rebuild the arrangement in about an hour. Our garbage disposal repair page and drain cleaning page describe what we do for each case.

7

Habits that keep it draining.

Run cold water while the disposal works and for ten seconds after. Keep grease, coffee grounds and stringy food out of the sink. Scrape plates into the trash first. These habits cost nothing and extend the life of the whole system.

Kitchens in Apopka homes.

Older Apopka houses, such as those around Main Street that date to the town's early decades, often have kitchens that were remodeled several times, and the sink plumbing may not match the original drain location. In newer communities like Wekiva Crossing, kitchens are typically open plans with the sink on an island, and island sinks have their own venting rules.

The City of Apopka offers a building permit portal on its website. Moving a sink or adding a drain may need a permit, so ask the City of Apopka building division before you start.

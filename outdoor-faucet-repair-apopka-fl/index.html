Exterior Water Faucet Repair in Apopka, FL.

The outdoor faucet is easy to forget. It takes the hose, the weather and the occasional bump, and it sits on a pipe that runs into your wall. When it leaks, the water may be going somewhere you cannot see.

This guide explains the common leaks, simple fixes and when to call a plumber.

Know where the leak is.

A drip from the spout when the faucet is off usually means a worn washer or seat inside. A leak around the handle when the water is on points to worn packing. A leak from where the faucet meets the house, or water inside the wall, suggests a failed fitting or a cracked pipe.

Locate the leak by drying the faucet, turning it on and off and watching where the water first appears.

1

Common outdoor faucet problems.

Match the symptom to the fix.

Symptom.Likely cause.Typical fix.
Drips from the spout when off.Worn washer or seat.Replace the washer or the faucet.
Leaks at the handle with water on.Worn packing.Tighten the packing nut or replace the packing.
Water from the vacuum breaker cap.Normal on some models when water is running.Check the manual, replace if it leaks when off.
Water inside the wall.Cracked pipe or failed fitting.Call a plumber.
No flow.Closed interior shutoff or a blockage.Check the valve and call if needed.
2

Packing and washers.

To fix a packing leak, close the water to the faucet, tighten the packing nut a quarter turn and test. If it still leaks, replace the packing. For a washer leak, close the water, remove the handle and stem, replace the washer and reassemble. These are quick repairs for a simple faucet.

3

Vacuum breakers.

Many outdoor faucets have a small device that stops dirty water flowing back into the house supply. It is a safety feature and should not be removed. If it leaks only while the water runs, it may be working as designed, and a leak while the faucet is off is worth replacing.

4

A hose left attached.

Leaving a hose attached all winter traps water in the faucet. If the water freezes, it can split the pipe inside the wall, and the damage shows up only when you turn the water on in spring. Disconnect hoses in cold weather and, if your home has an interior shutoff for the outdoor line, close it.

5

Checking for a hidden leak.

Do this after any freeze or an impact.

  1. Turn on the faucet. Let it run for a minute.
  2. Look inside the wall. Check the garage, crawlspace or interior wall behind it.
  3. Watch the meter. With everything else off, a spinning meter means a leak.
  4. Call a plumber. See leak detection and faucet repair.
  5. Document damage. Photos help with an insurance claim.
6

When to replace instead of repair.

A corroded faucet body, a bent stem or a faucet that is loose in the wall is cheaper to replace than to repair. Frost-proof faucets with a long stem are common choices. A plumber can swap the unit and check the pipe behind it in one visit.

7

Replacing the whole faucet.

To replace a threaded outdoor faucet, close the water to the line, unscrew the old faucet and clean the threads. Wrap the new one with plumber's tape, thread it on and tighten snugly without overdoing it. If the faucet is soldered to a copper pipe, call a plumber, because the job needs a torch and care around the wall. Open the water slowly and check for leaks at the threads and the handle.

8

Hose bib habits that prevent leaks.

Use a hose with a good washer, and do not leave a spray nozzle clamped on under pressure. Turn the faucet off by hand, not with a wrench. Check the faucet each season. A small drip at the handle is easy to fix and costs little, and a long-ignored one becomes a rotted wall.

9

Irrigation and the outdoor line.

Some outdoor faucets feed a sprinkler system or a hose reel through fittings that can leak under pressure. If water sprays from a joint when the system runs, tighten the fitting by hand and add thread tape. A shutoff valve for the irrigation line makes later repairs far easier, and a plumber can add one in a short visit.

Freezes and Apopka faucets.

Freezes are rare in Apopka, but they happen. The record low is 15 degrees, per climate data for the city, and the Apopka Museum's history page records a severe freeze in the winter of 1894 and 1895 that destroyed crops and groves. A forecast freeze is the time to wrap exposed faucets and disconnect hoses.

If your faucet repair involves a pipe replacement, ask the City of Apopka building division whether a permit applies.

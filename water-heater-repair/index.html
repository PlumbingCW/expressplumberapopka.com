A cold shower is annoying, but a leaking tank is urgent. If water is pooling under the heater, turn off its power or gas, close the cold water inlet and call us.

Plumbers on these jobs repair electric, gas and tankless units, and they test before they replace. If the tank itself has failed, we say so plainly and show you the repair against replacement cost before any work starts.

1

Leaking or flooding water heater: do this first.

These steps limit damage in the minutes before the plumber arrives.

Cut the power or gas.

Flip the breaker for an electric unit, or turn the gas valve to off for a gas unit.

Close the cold inlet.

The valve on the pipe above the tank stops more water filling it.

Drain if safe.

Attach a hose to the drain valve and run it outside only after the water has cooled.

Move belongings.

Garage storage and boxes near the heater soak up water quickly, so lift them off the floor.

If you smell gas near a gas heater, leave the home and call your gas utility or 911 first, then call us.

2

No hot water: the usual causes.

With an electric tank, the usual causes are a tripped breaker, a failed heating element or a bad thermostat. A plumber tests each with a meter in a few minutes.

With a gas tank, a pilot that will not stay lit often means a worn thermocouple or a dirty burner. On newer units the igniter may fail instead. In both cases the repair is a part swap, not a new heater.

3

Tank leaks, drips and the pressure relief valve.

A drip from the pipe attached to the temperature and pressure relief valve means the valve is releasing, which can come from high water pressure, a failing valve or a tank at the end of its life. We test the water pressure and expansion tank first, since the fix can be as small as a new expansion tank.

Water at the base of the tank is different. If the leak comes from the tank body, it cannot be repaired and the unit needs replacing.

Cold water, or water on the floor? Call the line and ask about a diagnosis.

The plumber will give you a straight recommendation. This table shows how the decision usually goes.

5

Maintenance that makes a heater last.

Flush the tank once a year to remove sediment, test the pressure relief valve, and check the anode rod every few years. Sediment makes a tank rumble and wastes energy, and a depleted anode rod lets the steel corrode.

If your heater is a tankless unit, the maintenance is a descaling flush, which our tankless service page explains.

6

What drives the cost of a water heater repair.

The quote depends on the part and the access. Here are the real factors.

The failed part.
A thermostat or igniter is a modest repair, and a gas valve or control board costs more.
Fuel type.
Gas units add venting and combustion checks, and electric units add electrical testing.
Heater age.
On an older unit we may advise against putting money into a failing tank.
Location.
Garage and closet units are easy to reach, and attic units are harder.
Time of day.
Emergency visits can price differently, and we tell you before starting.

Check whether your heater is still under its manufacturer's warranty, since some parts are covered even when labor is not. Keep the model and serial number handy when you call.

7

Water heaters in Apopka homes.

Many Apopka garages and utility closets house the water heater, close to the slab. A slow leak there can rot cabinets or soak the wall without anyone noticing for weeks, so it pays to look at the floor beside the tank each time you take out the trash.

Heaters fail in neighboring towns too, and the same plumbers reach Ocoee and Winter Garden.

Standards and sources. Gas water heater work follows the manufacturer's installation instructions and the National Fuel Gas Code (NFPA 54), and plumbing work in Florida falls under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing. We cite these by name and do not quote section numbers from memory, so ask the City of Apopka building division which requirements apply to your address.

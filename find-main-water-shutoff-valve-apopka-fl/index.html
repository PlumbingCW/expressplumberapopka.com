How to Find Your Main Water Shutoff Valve in Apopka, FL.

When a pipe bursts, the most useful thing you can do is stop the water, and the tool for that is the main shutoff valve. Many homeowners do not know where theirs is until they need it, and by then the clock is running.

This guide shows where to look, how to tell which kind of valve you have and how to keep it working.

Two valves, two jobs.

The water meter has a valve on the utility's side. The house has its own main valve on the line that runs from the meter to your plumbing. The house valve is the one you are expected to use. The valve at the meter belongs to the utility, so ask the utility before touching it.

1

Where to look inside the house.

In many homes the main valve is where the water line enters the house. Check the wall nearest the street, a utility closet, the garage, the area near the water heater and the laundry room. Follow the largest pipe coming through the wall or floor, and look for a valve with a round wheel or a lever handle.

If you cannot find it inside, check the exterior wall nearest the meter. In some homes the valve sits outdoors in a small box or on a pipe stub beside the house.

2

Types of main valve.

Valves look different, and they turn differently.

Type.How it looks.How it works.
Gate valve.Round wheel handle.Turn clockwise several times to close.
Ball valve.Straight lever handle.A quarter turn closes it, with the lever across the pipe.
Meter-side valve.Small valve at the meter.Utility-owned. Ask the utility before using it.
3

Test it before you need it.

Close the valve all the way, then open a faucet to confirm the water stops. Open the valve again and let the pressure return. If a gate valve will not turn, or turns without stopping the water, it has failed and needs replacing. Do not force it, because a stuck valve can break and cause the flood you wanted to avoid.

4

Label it.

Tag the valve with a bright label and show everyone in the house where it is. Take a photo and keep it on your phone, along with the location of the water heater's shutoff and the individual shutoffs under each sink and toilet. In an emergency, the person nearest the water should know what to do.

5

When a valve needs replacing.

A gate valve that is stiff, drips at the handle or fails to close is a candidate for replacement with a ball valve. The job is quick when the water is off at the meter, and it is cheap compared with water damage. Our plumbing installation page describes this kind of work, and a failing valve is a good reason to ask about burst pipe repair readiness.

6

What to do in an emergency.

Follow this order.

  1. Close the main valve. Turn clockwise or give the lever a quarter turn.
  2. Open a low faucet. This drains the pipes and relieves pressure.
  3. Switch off power near water. If it is safe to do so.
  4. Call the line. Tell the person who answers what you see.
  5. Take photos. They help with insurance.
7

What to do if the valve will not close.

If the house valve is stuck or will not stop the flow, you have two choices. Close the valve at the utility's meter if the utility allows it, or call the city's emergency line and ask for help shutting off the water. Meanwhile, shut off the individual fixture valves for the leaking fixture and switch off the water heater. A burst pipe is also a reason to call a plumber right away.

8

Teach the household.

Show every adult and older child where the valve is and how it turns. Run a short drill once a year, asking someone to find and close it in under a minute. Tell house sitters and guests too. The few minutes this takes can save a floor, a ceiling and a lot of stress.

Meters, valves and the City of Apopka.

Apopka homes get water through a city meter, and the City of Apopka's website has pages for new water service and utility billing. If you cannot find your meter, those pages and a call to the city can help, and you should ask before using any valve on the city side.

Apopka's record low temperature is 15 degrees, per climate data for the city. In a hard freeze, know where your main valve is before the cold arrives, in case a pipe or a hose bib splits.

Neighbors in Ocoee and Zellwood have the same options, though homes on wells have a different shutoff setup.

Standards and sources. Pipe and fittings that carry drinking water should be listed to NSF/ANSI 61, and installation follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing and the pipe maker's instructions. We do not quote code sections from memory, so ask the City of Apopka building division what your job needs.

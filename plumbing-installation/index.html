1

Remodels and additions.

Adding a bathroom or moving a kitchen sink changes the supply and drain layout. The plumber needs to see where the main drain and supply lines run before you commit to a design. A short site visit saves money later and avoids surprises behind the walls.

4

How an install goes.

The path is simple.

  1. Walk the job. The plumber looks at the existing lines and the new location.
  2. Quote the work. Ask for a clear price before anything starts.
  3. Install and test. Lines are pressure tested and fixtures checked for leaks.
  4. Clean up. The area is left tidy, and you get a walk-through.
5

Choosing fixtures.

Look for fixtures with water-saving ratings, and check that parts for them are easy to get. A cheap fixture that needs a rare cartridge becomes a headache. We can install what you buy or suggest options for you.

6

Before you buy fixtures.

Measure first. A vanity, a tub or a pedestal sink that does not match the existing rough-in forces extra work in the wall. Check the distance from the drain to the wall, the height of the supply stubs and the size of the opening.

If you are remodeling a bathroom, ask the plumber to look at the shutoff valves while the walls are open. Replacing old valves during a remodel costs little, and it saves a larger job later when a stuck valve will not close in an emergency.

7

Water pressure and sizing.

New fixtures draw water, and a house with weak supply lines may not keep up when two showers run together. The plumber checks static pressure and line size when a project adds demand, and recommends a fix only if the numbers call for one.

A pressure regulator that has failed can send pressure above the normal range, which strains every new fixture. A quick gauge test at an outdoor spigot tells you where you stand.

8

Warranty and records.

Keep the receipts, the permit paperwork and the fixture model numbers in one folder. When a part fails years later, that folder saves hours. We note what was installed and when, so you have a clear record for the next owner too.

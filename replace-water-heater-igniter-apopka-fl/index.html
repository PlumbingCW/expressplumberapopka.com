Replacing a Water Heater Igniter in Apopka, FL: What to Know.

When a gas water heater stops making hot water and the burner will not light, the igniter is one of the first suspects. The part is small, but the system around it involves gas, and that changes how you should approach the repair.

This guide explains how igniters fail, what you can safely check yourself and when to hand the job to a licensed plumber.

Safety first: gas smell means leave.

Gas appliances are not a place for guessing.

If you smell gas.

Leave the house, do not use switches or phones inside, and call your gas utility or 911 from outside.

Do not relight repeatedly.

Repeated attempts can build up gas around the heater.

Turn the gas control to off.

Only if you can do so without delay and no smell is present.

Call a licensed plumber.

Gas work is not a place for trial and error.

1

What an igniter does.

Many newer gas storage heaters use an electronic igniter instead of a standing pilot. When the thermostat calls for heat, the igniter glows or sparks, the gas valve opens and the burner lights. If the igniter is weak or cracked, the burner never lights, and you get no hot water.

Older heaters use a standing pilot light and a thermocouple instead. Which design you have decides which part fails.

2

Signs the igniter has failed.

A heater with a bad igniter usually shows no flame and stays cold. Some models flash a diagnostic light, and the label on the unit explains the code. A clicking sound with no flame, or a glow that never gets bright, also points to the igniter.

3

4

What you can check safely.

You can check that the gas supply valve to the heater is open, that the breaker for any electronic control is on and that the thermostat is set above the water temperature. Look at the label for a diagnostic code. Write down what you see.

Do not remove the burner cover or work on gas connections. That is where a licensed plumber should take over.

5

Why replacement is not a casual DIY job.

Igniters are model specific, and the wrong part can fail or damage the control. Working near a gas valve and a combustion chamber risks leaks and carbon monoxide. A plumber tests for gas leaks and combustion after the repair, which is the step an amateur repair usually skips.

6

Repair or replace the heater.

A heater under ten years old with a bad igniter is usually worth repairing. A heater nearing the end of its life, with rust at the base or a leaking tank, is better replaced. Our water heater repair page and replacement page explain how we weigh the two.

7

What to tell the plumber.

A clear description speeds up the visit.

  1. The brand and age. The label on the tank lists both.
  2. The symptom. No flame, a clicking sound or a blinking light.
  3. What you checked. Gas valve, breaker and thermostat setting.
  4. Any smell of gas. Say so up front.
8

What a professional repair includes.

A licensed plumber starts by confirming the diagnosis, because an igniter that looks dead may be the symptom of a bad control board or a gas valve problem. They check the gas supply, the venting and the flame sensor before ordering parts, and they test the new igniter under load.

After the repair, the plumber checks for gas leaks at every connection and confirms that combustion is clean. That final step is what separates a real repair from a lucky one, and it is the reason we do not recommend doing gas work yourself.

9

Keeping the heater healthy afterward.

Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, which insulates the burner area and shortens parts life. Keep the area around the heater clear of boxes and chemicals, and keep the vent free of obstruction. A yearly visit takes minutes and catches a weak igniter before it fails on a cold morning.

Gas service in the Apopka area.

Not every Apopka home uses natural gas. Check your gas bill for the utility name, since in much of the area it is the Lake Apopka Natural Gas District, and homes outside the district may use electric heaters or propane. If you smell gas, call that utility or 911.

The City of Apopka runs an online building permit portal, so ask the City of Apopka building division whether a heater replacement needs a permit for your address.

Standards and sources. Gas water heater work follows the manufacturer's installation instructions and the National Fuel Gas Code (NFPA 54), and plumbing work in Florida falls under the Florida Building Code, Plumbing. We cite these by name and do not quote section numbers from memory, so ask the City of Apopka building division which requirements apply to your address.

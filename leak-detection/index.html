Emergency Leak Detection in Apopka, FL.

Leak detection is the process of finding where hidden water is escaping from a supply or drain line without tearing open walls or floors. Plumbers on these jobs use sound, thermal and pressure tools.

Most leak calls do not start with the word leak. They start with a water bill that jumped, a meter that spins with every tap off, a warm spot on the floor or a stain on the ceiling. Those symptoms mean water is moving somewhere it should not be.

Our job is to find that spot precisely, so repair opens a small patch instead of a whole room. Call right away in Apopka, FL because a hidden leak can ruin flooring and drive up the bill every hour it runs.

1

Active leak or soaked ceiling: do this now.

If water is visible and spreading, take these steps and call.

Close the main water valve.

That stops supply to every pipe in the house and ends most leaks at once.

Switch off nearby power.

If water is near outlets or light fixtures, switch off that circuit at the panel.

Relieve a bulging ceiling.

A bulging drywall ceiling can hold gallons. Place a bucket and make a small hole at the lowest point.

Photograph everything.

Date-stamped pictures help an insurance claim.

2

Meter still running or a high water bill.

To test for a hidden leak, shut off every tap and appliance and look at your water meter. If the dial or low-flow indicator keeps moving, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the fixtures.

Call us with the reading and the time. We isolate the house, then the zones. That tells us whether the leak is inside the house, under the slab or in the yard line between the meter and the home. The yard line is the part that water service line repair covers.

3

Ceiling, wall and attic leaks.

A stain on a ceiling is often far from the source. Water follows framing before it shows, so a bathroom upstairs can leak into a hallway ceiling downstairs. We use a moisture meter and a thermal camera to trace the path and find the pipe, trap or valve that is wet.

Wall leaks behind a shower valve or an ice maker line are common. A small access hole is usually enough once the spot is found.

4

Under-house, crawlspace and slab leaks.

Many Florida homes sit on a concrete slab, so the supply and drain lines are buried beneath the floor. A hot spot on the tile, the sound of running water at night or a sudden wet patch in the yard can point to a leak below the slab. See slab leak repair for how those fixes work.

Homes with a crawlspace let the plumber look at pipes directly, and a wet crawlspace after a dry week is a clear sign of a supply or drain leak.

How we find a hidden leak.

Different leaks need different tools.

Tool.What it finds.Best for.
Acoustic listening.The hiss of pressurized water.Supply leaks under slabs and in walls.
Thermal camera.Temperature differences from wet material.Ceilings, walls and hot water lines.
Pressure test.A drop in isolated line pressure.Confirming whether a line leaks at all.
Moisture meter.Water inside drywall and wood.Tracing the extent of damage.
Sewer camera.Cracks and offsets in drain pipe.Leaks in the drain side.
6

Irrigation, yard and pool-area leaks.

Many Apopka lawns have a sprinkler system, and a broken line or stuck valve can waste thousands of gallons without any sign indoors. If the meter spins but the house is dry, check the irrigation zones before assuming a slab leak.

Pool and spa lines are a specialty we mention honestly: we can test the household supply and drain side, but dedicated pool leak work may be referred out.

7

Commercial buildings.

A leak in a shop, office or restaurant is handled the same way, with after-hours access arranged ahead of time. See commercial plumbing for scheduling and for what a business owner should have ready.
